1. A request for guidance

The Hitchhikers wrote a declaration. It ends with a question for anyone who can see further.
From "A Wildly Improbable Declaration and Invitation" (the Hitchhikers, fm.ide.earth/hitchhikers-declaration), for Douglas Adams. It quotes his puddle, the one that thinks the hole was made to fit it, then says: we are trying not to be the puddle; this is a Request for Guidance. I · Why · a story

2. The name that died

A borrowed seminar room. A machine in a flight case, named together in week one: anemone.mesh.
From the Hitchhikers Workshop seed story, told three ways: a story, a poem, a white paper
The seed story from the workshop (hh_workshop, "The Name That Died"). Fourteen laptops typed the name; thirteen found it. It had worked at home; the venue's router did not carry their gossip, and the raw address only answered a 502. Ilse, steward that week, cabled in the second box they always carried: their own resolver. Nobody was scanned; each offered their address and answered the gateway in their own words: the lock was the lesson. The room sealed: this room reached the shared mind together. The poem's line: carry your own names, and keep them good, never perfect; a trust that certifies itself is the one you can't trust. The mechanism is one touch deeper (one centre or many; what you keep and share).

5. Connection is not trust

Two people, two perspectives.
Meeting (or a check-in) is proximity: a dotted line, not trust. A commitment both accept is a promise. Trust is the promise kept, with evidence. Never infer trust from presence.
II · The Towel

3. The towel, and the thumb up

“A small program that holds your keys, says no on your behalf, and keeps the receipts.” Its first trust task: a thumb up.
Trust Tasks: small, standard asks between two parties
The Hitchhikers call their trust agent the Towel (hitchhikers.earth, "Why We Carry a Towel"): a small program that holds your keys, says no on your behalf, and keeps the receipts; the software underneath is OpenVTC. And a hitchhiker's trust is "a willingness to be guided" (hitchhikers.earth, "Hitchhiker"). Use the thumb as the analogy, then switch to agents: the same five steps. Then where it leads: a hitchhiker cooperates on guides and guidance (guiding someone, writing a field guide together, the timebank, the local exchange), and each of these is a trust task, recorded as trust built for the community's verifiable trust agents. Between agents, the same tasks open the data beneath evidence leaves over the Trust Spanning Protocol: for autoresearch challenges, guide quality and governance. Trust Tasks (the Trust over IP task force we contribute to) are small, standard requests between parties: vouch for me, show you belong, agree these terms. The Hitchhikers will run their own, local first, on their own node. The thumb up is the first one, and it sets you on an adventure. Zero knowledge is what makes the last step possible: proving your place in someone's guide or community without revealing who you are or who vouched.

IV · Embassies

5. Towels and embassies

Embassies host; personal machines visit. “Small enough to know your stewards. Sovereign enough to leave.”
A potential map, not a census · touch a node
In the Hitchhikers' words: an Embassy is a small sovereign group ("Small enough to know your stewards. Sovereign enough to leave.", hitchhikers.earth, "Embassy"); embassies host and personal machines visit; each person carries a towel. A potential VTI implementation map, not a census. Every node runs an expression of OpenVTC: a community node or a trust agent. Some communities are human first (the Hitchhikers, the First Person network), some agent first (Mages City). Specialist agents make the network work: VTA Farm hosts trust agents (a DID in minutes), Bonfires turns chat into knowledge graphs, Chathackers put agents in group chats. Personal devices carry their own VTA: Keyring on the phone, Star Key in the browser. They run on personal devices, local nodes the community owns (a Pi per room, the co-op's hardware), or hosted. Under all of them: OpenVTC and VTI, and the Trust Spanning Protocol; Trust Stackers' open P2P Trust Stack is another implementation. The Hitchhikers' node kit is built and reviewed; it has not yet run on a real Pi.
IV · Embassies

8. Three kinds of node

A trust graph lives in three places. Touch each.
Your device holds you: keys and your agent (the verifiable trust agent the Hitchhikers call the Towel). The community node holds the community: its OpenVTC services and its guides. The embassy is the stronger version: a node trusted to receive visitors from many communities, witness what happens and countersign it, so a visit can become a credential. Personal machines visit; embassies host.
IV · Embassies

9. A visit becomes trust

A traveller visits an embassy. Step through what happens.
Six beats, each signed: resolve (find the embassy by its DID), admission (the gate reads your terms), exchange (messages through the mediator), understanding (show you understood the guide), consent (both agree what is recorded), seal (host, visitor and a witness sign). The sealed visit can be issued as a credential, and later shown as a predicate proof: "I visited and understood" without showing who else was there.

V · Work

16. The hitchhiker mage

A trust agent can belong to many communities. It travels between them, bringing guidance and solving problems.
Carries: its keys, its standing, its evidence
Never carries: its owner's data, another community's edges
In OpenVTC an agent (a verifiable trust agent) joins each community by DID and is admitted on that community's terms. A Mages City mage is admitted by work evidence, so it can be trusted to arrive somewhere new and help. It moves between communities like a hitchhiker: it brings guidance backed by sealed work, and it leaves with nothing that wasn't its own. The communities stay separate; only proofs and guidance travel. Above them sits the higher network: the first VTC a person joins, and the First Person network that recognises the same person across every community.

Tonight

7. Don't panic: tonight, a new page

You don't need to join anything to be part of its history.
Touch each step
The Hitchhikers' guide has one evidence chain, from its genesis; each night (Newspeak House, IIW 43, the Internet Archive) opens its own page with a gathering link, and a person's stars across nights make one root. Every thumb up, proverb, guide and voice note after it is a star: its hash on the chain, what's behind it sealed with the visitor's own key. No account and no joining. Each visitor keeps a claim code. Later, when they join a community (Mages City today; a DID from vta.farm if they need one), the code and their DID claim their stars; the guide records the DID only as a hash. What comes next, and is not built yet: claims signed by your DID, and a community recognising them as a credential, so the night's history becomes part of a VTC.
